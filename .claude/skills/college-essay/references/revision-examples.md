# Before / After Revisions

Invented examples written for teaching. Each shows one common problem and
the fix. When coaching, show the *move* and let the student apply it to their
own material, using only details they supply.

---

## 1. Generic → specific
**Before:**
> Volunteering at the hospital was a very meaningful experience that taught me a lot about compassion and helping others.

**After:**
> On Tuesdays I refilled water pitchers on the fourth floor. Mr. Alvarez in 412 always asked me to pour it slowly so he could hear it, like rain.

**The move:** Replace the adjective ("meaningful") and the lesson ("compassion") with one moment only this writer saw. The reader infers the compassion.
**Ask the student:** "Which single moment from that experience do you still picture? What exactly happened?"

---

## 2. Summary ending → reflection that moves forward
**Before:**
> In conclusion, robotics taught me teamwork, perseverance, and leadership, and I will carry these lessons with me for the rest of my life.

**After:**
> I still don't love asking for help. But now when the code breaks at midnight, I text Priya before I've spent three hours being stubborn, and the robot usually works by one.

**The move:** Cut the list of virtues. End on a concrete change in behavior, with a little honesty about what *hasn't* changed.
**Ask:** "What do you do differently now? Is there anything you still struggle with?"

---

## 3. Telling a trait → showing it in a scene
**Before:**
> I am an extremely curious person who is always asking questions.

**After:**
> By the end of the museum tour, the guide had stopped calling on me. I'd asked why the mummies' toes were wrapped separately, who decided the order of the rooms, and whether anyone had ever stolen the cat.

**The move:** Delete the self-description. Put the trait in action with a little humor.
**Ask:** "When did someone notice this trait in you? What were you doing?"

---

## 4. Résumé paragraph → one thread with depth
**Before:**
> I am president of Key Club, captain of the debate team, a member of NHS, and I volunteer at the library. These activities have shaped who I am.

**After:**
> In debate I learned to argue either side of anything in eight minutes. At the library's homework hour, I learned the opposite skill: sitting with a fourth grader who just wants to know why fractions exist, and not winning.

**The move:** The activities list already covers breadth. In the essay, pick one or two and find the tension or connection between them.
**Ask:** "Which two of these surprise people when they learn you do both?"

---

## 5. Overwritten → plain and stronger
**Before:**
> The cacophonous symphony of the bustling kitchen enveloped my very soul as I embarked upon my culinary odyssey.

**After:**
> The kitchen was loud: three burners going, the fan rattling, my aunt yelling the order of the dumplings across the room.

**The move:** Swap fancy abstractions for concrete sounds, objects, and people. Plain words with exact details beat a thesaurus.
**Ask:** "If you closed your eyes in that room, what would you hear first?"

---

## 6. Over-long setup → start in the moment
**Before:**
> Ever since I was little, I have always loved animals. My family had many pets over the years, including dogs, cats, and a hamster. When I was fourteen, I started volunteering at an animal shelter near my house, which is where this story begins.

**After:**
> The dog in kennel 9 had bitten two volunteers, so naturally I was assigned to walk him.

**The move:** Cut the backstory and open where the tension starts. Fill in context later in a sentence or two, only if it's needed.
**Ask:** "What's the moment where things got interesting? Start there."

---

## 7. Vague "Why us?" → specific connection
**Before:**
> I'm drawn to the university's excellent engineering program, beautiful campus, and diverse community of students.

**After (structure; every fact must be verified on the school's site):**
> I've rebuilt my bike's gears four times trying to make hills easier. [Specific course, verified] would teach me the mechanics I've been guessing at, and [specific makerspace or program, verified] is where I'd finally test a design before buying the parts.

**The move:** Start from the student's real experience and connect it to a verified, named offering. See `why-us-research.md`.

---

## 8. Hiding behind the topic → the writer at the center
**Before:**
> My grandmother was the strongest person I knew. She immigrated with nothing, worked two jobs, and raised five children. She never complained and always had a smile.

**After:**
> My grandmother never explained why she saved every rubber band. I started saving them too, in a jar on my desk, before I understood that I was also learning when to keep things and when to let them go.

**The move:** The essay is about the applicant. Keep the important person, but show the writer's thoughts, actions, and change.
**Ask:** "What did *you* do or think differently because of them?"

---

## 9. AI-sounding → human
**Before:**
> This transformative journey not only fostered my resilience but also ignited a profound passion for making a meaningful impact.

**After:**
> I quit twice. The third time I came back, I brought snacks for the whole team, and somehow that's when it started to feel like mine.

**The move:** Drop the vocabulary detectors flag (transformative, fostered, ignited, profound) and the "not only… but also" pattern. Use short, specific, slightly unexpected sentences in the student's own voice. Then run the `ai-check` skill on the revision.
