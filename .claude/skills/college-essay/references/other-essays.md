# Other Essay Types

Confirm current limits and wording in each application or school portal.

---

## Common App "Additional Information" (300 words max for first-year applicants)
For context the rest of the application can't show. Optional; leave it blank if
there's nothing to explain.

Good uses:
- A grade dip and why (illness, family crisis, a move, a death)
- Unusual school circumstances: no APs offered, block schedule, homeschooling, school closure
- Significant family responsibilities or work hours
- An activity or project that needs more explanation than 150 characters
- A disability or learning difference, if the student wants to share it

How to write it:
- Factual, direct, brief. Bullet points are fine.
- State the circumstance, its effect (dates, grades, activities), and what's true now.
- No pity, no repeating the essay, no overflow from the personal statement.

> Example structure: "In spring of 10th grade I was hospitalized for six weeks with [condition]. I completed coursework remotely, which is why my chemistry grade dropped to a C. I've been fully recovered since August; my junior-year science grades reflect my usual work."

### "Challenges and Circumstances" (about 250 words, per recent guides)
Recent guides describe this as replacing the former COVID-19/community-disruption
question, for challenges like housing or food insecurity, caregiving, health, or
access to technology. Confirm the current wording in the portal. Same approach as
Additional Information: concise, factual, and only what the student chooses to share.

## Scholarship essays
- **Read what the funder values** (leadership, service, financial need, a field of study, a community) and answer that directly. Readers often score against a rubric.
- Be concrete about goals: what you'll study, why, and what you'll do with it.
- If need is part of it, be honest and specific without melodrama. Show what you've done with what you had.
- **Reuse wisely:** a strong personal statement can often be adapted, but always reshape the opening and ending around *this* prompt.
- Follow every instruction exactly (length, format, file type). Scholarship committees disqualify for less.
- Keep a tracker (deadline, amount, prompt, status) in the profile.

## Letter of Continued Interest (waitlisted or deferred)
Purpose: tell the school it's still your top choice (if true) and share real updates.

Structure (300–500 words, unless the school says otherwise):
1. Thank them and reaffirm interest. If it's genuinely their first choice and they'd attend if admitted, say so clearly. **Only say this to one school.**
2. **Substantive updates since applying:** new grades, awards, leadership, project results, test scores.
3. One or two *new* specific reasons the school fits (not repeated from the "Why us?" essay).
4. Brief, gracious close.

Rules:
- Check the school's waitlist/deferral instructions first. Some say not to send anything, or require an online form.
- Professional and upbeat. No complaining, no pressure, no repeating the whole application.
- Send once, at the time they suggest. Don't flood the office.

## Transfer essays
The main question: **why transfer, and why this school now?**
- Explain what you're looking for that your current school can't offer (a specific major, research, program, environment) without trashing your current school.
- Show what you've done in college: courses, grades, activities, work, and how they've clarified your goals.
- Connect to specific, verified offerings at the target school (see `why-us-research.md`).
- Show maturity and a clear academic plan. Transfer readers care about direction.
- The Common App for transfers has its own prompt and limits (Additional Information is character-limited for transfers). Check the portal.

## Short-answer and "quirky" prompts
(University of Chicago, Stanford roommate letter, list prompts, 50-word answers)
- Personality and specificity are the point. Playful is fine; trying to impress isn't.
- In 50–100 words, make one point with one detail. No warm-up.
- Lists: be honest and specific. Unexpected but genuine picks reveal more than impressive ones.
