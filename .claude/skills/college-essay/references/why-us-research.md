# "Why Us?" Research Workflow

A "Why us?" essay has two halves: specific facts about the school, and the
student's specific reasons those facts matter to them. Most weak ones have the
first half from a brochure and skip the second.

## 1. Gather the student side first
From the profile (`essays/profile.md`) or by asking:
- Intended major(s) and the questions or problems they care about.
- 2–3 things they've actually done related to those interests (projects, jobs, clubs, reading).
- How they like to learn: small seminars, research, hands-on, interdisciplinary, co-ops.
- What they want outside class: a specific activity, community, or tradition.
- Anything genuine they've seen or heard: a campus visit, a conversation with a student, an info session moment.

## 2. Research the school
Use web search/fetch when available. Prefer primary sources:

| Look for | Where |
|---|---|
| Courses (number + title) | Online course catalog / bulletin |
| Majors, tracks, special curricula | Department pages, "requirements" pages |
| Professors and their research | Faculty directory, lab websites |
| Research programs for undergrads | Undergraduate research office, named programs |
| Unique structures | Core curriculum, open curriculum, co-op, residential colleges, January term |
| Clubs and student orgs | Student activities directory |
| Traditions and campus culture | Student newspaper, school blog, admissions blog |
| Facilities | Specific centers, makerspaces, field stations, museums |

**Accuracy rules, which are non-negotiable:**
- **Never invent a course, professor, program, or club.** If you can't verify it on a current school page, don't put it in the essay. Say "I couldn't verify X" instead.
- Record the source URL for every fact in the research table so the student can check it.
- Check that it's current: professors move, courses get retired, programs close. Prefer pages updated in the last year or two.
- Copy proper nouns exactly (names, course numbers, program titles) and spell them the same way every time.
- If web access isn't available, give the student a research checklist instead of guessing from memory.

## 3. Build the research table
Save to `essays/schools/<school>.md`:

| School offering (specific) | Source URL | Verified on | Connects to (student's past) | What they'd do with it (future) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

Aim for 6–10 rows, then pick the **3–4 strongest connections**. Depth beats a list.

## 4. Write it
- Lead with the student, not the school: a moment, question, or project that leads naturally into what the school offers.
- For each offering: name it, then spend most of the words on *why it matters to them* and *what they'd do*.
- Show they'd contribute, not just take: what they bring to a club, lab, or classroom.
- **Swap test:** replace the school name with a peer school's. If the essay still works, it isn't specific enough.
- Cut prestige, rankings, weather, beauty, generic "diverse community," and anything copied from the admissions site's slogans.
- Don't name-drop a professor without a real reason. One well-explained connection beats three names.

## 5. Reusing material across schools
Students can reuse their *own* half (interests, experiences) across "Why us?"
essays, but redo the school half every time. Before submitting, search the
draft for every other school's name; a wrong school name is a common and
costly mistake.
